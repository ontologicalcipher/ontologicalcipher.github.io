FUNDANICS STELLAR DISTANCE SCALING FORMULA

SDSF — Stellar Distance Scaling Formula

Status: Mathematically verified • Scientifically unvalidated

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. PROPOSED EQUATION

R = D₍star₎ × [D₍Sun–Earth₎ ÷ D₍Moon–Earth₎]

Where:

R = resulting scaled distance
D₍star₎ = distance from Earth to the selected star
D₍Sun–Earth₎ = mean Earth–Sun distance
D₍Moon–Earth₎ = mean Earth–Moon distance

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

2. ORIGIN OF THE 389.17 FACTOR

The International Astronomical Union defines the astronomical unit (au) as exactly:

1 au = 149,597,870.7 km

The mean Earth–Moon distance is approximately:

384,400 km

Therefore:

149,597,870.7 ÷ 384,400 ≈ 389.17

So the proposed relation can be written as:

R ≈ 389.17 × D₍star₎

The value 389.17 is therefore a calculated dimensionless ratio, not a fundamental constant of nature.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

3. EXAMPLE — PROXIMA CENTAURI

Approximate distance:

D₍star₎ ≈ 4.246 light-years

Applying SDSF:

R = 4.246 × 389.17

R ≈ 1,652.7 light-years

The calculation is mathematically consistent because 389.17 is dimensionless.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

4. WHAT HAS BEEN VERIFIED?

✓ Mathematical verification

The ratio:

149,597,870.7 ÷ 384,400 ≈ 389.17

is correct to the stated precision.

✓ Dimensional consistency

Because the Sun–Earth distance divided by the Moon–Earth distance is dimensionless, multiplying it by a stellar distance preserves the distance unit.

✓ Astronomical reference values

The Earth–Moon average distance used here is consistent with NASA's published value of approximately 384,400 km.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

5. WHAT HAS NOT BEEN VERIFIED?

The calculation does not establish a new astronomical law, constant, or physical mechanism.

SDSF currently performs a linear rescaling of a known stellar distance by approximately 389.17.

No claim is made here that the resulting number represents a previously known physical quantity.

For SDSF to have deeper scientific significance, it would need to produce a testable prediction or independently reproducible relationship that cannot be explained simply by the mathematical scaling itself.

7. CONCLUSION

The FUNDANICS Stellar Distance Scaling Formula (SDSF) is mathematically valid as a distance-scaling relation:

R = D₍star₎ × [D₍Sun–Earth₎ ÷ D₍Moon–Earth₎]

or approximately:

R ≈ 389.17 × D₍star₎

The numerical foundation of the equation checks out.

However, mathematical validity is not the same as physical discovery.

At present, SDSF should be presented as a proposed mathematical relation for investigation, not as an established scientific law.
