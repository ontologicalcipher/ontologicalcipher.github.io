FUNDANICS RESONANT DECOUPLING THEORY

A proposed framework for controlling effective quantum coupling through resonant driving

01 — THE CORE IDEA

FRDT asks a specific question:

Can a precisely controlled periodic stimulus modify the effective coupling between components of a quantum system strongly enough to create a controllable low-coupling state?

The proposed sequence is:

External drive → Resonant interaction → Modified quantum dynamics → Effective coupling changes → Possible controlled decoupling

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

02 — THE MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION

A periodically driven quantum system can be represented by:

H(t) = H₀ + V(t)

For a sinusoidal drive:

V(t) = A cos(ωt)

where:

H₀ = undriven Hamiltonian
A = driving amplitude
ω = driving angular frequency

In suitable periodically driven two-state or tunnelling models, the effective coupling can be modified by the drive.

A commonly occurring form is:

Jₑff ≈ J × J₀(A/ℏω)

where:

J = original coupling
Jₑff = effective coupling under the drive
J₀ = zeroth-order Bessel function

For particular drive parameters, the Bessel factor can approach zero:

J₀(A/ℏω) ≈ 0

giving:

Jₑff ≈ 0

Within the applicable model and approximation, the effective coupling can therefore be strongly suppressed.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

03 — WHAT IS ALREADY ESTABLISHED?

This mathematical mechanism is not itself a new discovery.

Floquet engineering uses periodic driving to engineer effective Hamiltonians and modify quantum dynamics. Experiments with ultracold atoms and other quantum systems have demonstrated controlled changes in effective tunnelling and interactions.

Therefore:

Periodic driving → modified effective quantum dynamics

is established physics.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

04 — WHERE FRDT PROPOSES SOMETHING FURTHER

FRDT proposes investigating whether this established principle can be developed into a general strategy for controlled reduction of a selected quantum coupling.

The important distinction is:

FRDT does not claim that any atomic or nuclear force can simply be switched off.

Instead, it asks whether a suitable system, drive frequency, amplitude, phase, and timescale could produce a deliberately engineered low-coupling regime.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

05 — THEORETICAL LIMIT

The equation does not automatically apply to every physical interaction.

The effective-coupling expression depends on the specific Hamiltonian, approximations, driving regime, and physical system.

Periodically driven systems can also absorb energy and heat, which can limit how long engineered dynamics remain controllable.

Therefore:

Jₑff ≈ 0

must never be interpreted as:

“All physical forces become zero.”

It means that a particular effective coupling in a particular model can be suppressed under appropriate conditions.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

06 — THE FRDT HYPOTHESIS

The proposed extension is:

If a physical system can be identified in which an experimentally controllable interaction behaves as an effective coupling, then periodic driving may provide a method for dynamically reducing that coupling within a defined parameter regime.

The next question is whether such a mechanism can be designed for increasingly complex atomic systems.

08 — WHAT WOULD TEST FRDT?

A future test would need to define:

1. A specific quantum system

2. The exact interaction/coupling

3. Driving frequency

4. Driving amplitude

5. Phase and waveform

6. Predicted effective coupling

7. Duration of the engineered state

8. A measurable experimental signature

The result would then need to be compared against the undriven system and known effects such as ordinary resonance, heating, excitation, and decoherence.

CONCLUSION

FRDT is built on a real phenomenon:

Periodic driving can modify effective quantum dynamics.

The proposed step is to investigate whether this can be deliberately developed into a broader method for controlled reduction of selected quantum couplings.

The mathematics demonstrates that such coupling modification is possible in specific models.

It does not prove universal atomic or nuclear decoupling.

That part remains open for future theoretical and experimental work.

The mathematics defines the possibility.
The experiment defines the reality.
