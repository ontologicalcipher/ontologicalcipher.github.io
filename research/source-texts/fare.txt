01 — THE CORE IDEA

FARE asks:

Can the measurable response of an atomic system to an external frequency be used not only to observe the atom, but to continuously control its quantum state or interaction?

The proposed cycle is:

Frequency → Atomic response → Measurement → Feedback → Frequency adjustment → New response

The key idea is to turn resonance into a control variable.

02 — THE MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION

For a simplified two-level atomic system, the Hamiltonian can be represented as:

H = (ℏΔ/2)σᶻ + (ℏΩ/2)σˣ

where:

Δ = detuning between the applied frequency and the atomic transition

Ω = drive strength

ℏ = reduced Planck constant

The generalized Rabi frequency is:

Ωₑff = √(Ω² + Δ²)

For an ideal two-level system, the transition probability can be written as:

P(t) = [Ω²/(Ω² + Δ²)] sin²(Ωₑff t / 2)

Therefore, changing the driving frequency changes Δ, which changes the predicted response of the atom.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

03 — THE RESONANCE PRINCIPLE

When the applied frequency approaches the relevant transition frequency:

Δ → 0

the drive can strongly couple to the transition.

When the system is significantly detuned:

|Δ| ≫ Ω

the transition probability is strongly reduced in this simplified model.

Therefore:

Frequency → Detuning → Quantum response

is a mathematically defined relationship.

This is established quantum physics, not a new claim.

FARE proposes adding a feedback loop.

↓

This creates a closed-loop control concept.

Quantum measurement and feedback are established methods for manipulating and stabilizing quantum systems.

05 — THE CONTROL MODEL

A simplified feedback relationship can be written as:

e(t) = R(t) − y(t)

where:

R(t) = desired response

y(t) = measured atomic response

e(t) = control error

A controller can then adjust the driving parameters:

u(t) = F[e(t)]

where:

u(t) = applied control

F = chosen control law

The exact control law would depend on the physical system.

This is a framework for investigation—not a universal equation for atoms.

07 — AN IMPORTANT QUANTUM LIMIT

Control through repeated intervention does not automatically mean that a quantum system will become “frozen.”

The quantum Zeno effect demonstrates that sufficiently frequent measurements can inhibit certain quantum transitions. This has been experimentally observed in trapped ions.

But quantum systems can also exhibit the anti-Zeno effect, where repeated interventions accelerate rather than suppress a transition.

Therefore:

More control ≠ automatically less motion.

The timing, measurement process, coupling, and system dynamics determine the result.
