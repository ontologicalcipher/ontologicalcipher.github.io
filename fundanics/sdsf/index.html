FUNDANICS · Research note 001

Stellar Distance
Scaling Formula

An exploratory mathematical relationship proposed for examination. This page separates the formula's arithmetic from the much bigger question of whether it describes a measurable physical relationship.

HYPOTHESIS · NOT VALIDATED
01 / Proposed relationship

The formula

R = Dstar × (DSun–Earth ÷ DMoon–Earth)

Using the mean reference distances in kilometres, the scale factor is approximately 389.17.

DSun–Earth = 149,597,870.7 km (1 astronomical unit). DMoon–Earth = 384,400 km (mean distance). R has the same distance unit as Dstar.

Worked example

Proxima Centauri

Using the approximate distance of 4.246 light-years as an input:

4.246 × 389.17 ≈ 1,652.7 ly

This is the formula's output, not a newly measured distance or evidence that the scaling factor has a physical interpretation.

03 / Scientific assessment

What the calculation establishes

Established arithmetic
  • The ratio of the two stated reference distances is about 389.17.
  • Multiplying a distance by a dimensionless ratio preserves its distance unit.
  • The calculator reproduces the proposed relationship for supplied inputs.
Not established
  • The calculation does not independently measure stellar distance.
  • It does not show that the factor predicts a new observable quantity.
  • No new physical law or constant follows from the arithmetic alone.

For SDSF to become a useful scientific model, it needs a clearly defined observable that can be obtained independently, predictions that differ from existing methods, and tests against measurements not used to create the formula.
